If a police case (FIR) has been registered against you

General steps people in Kadapa usually take. Your own situation may need a different approach, so speak to an advocate early.

  1. Collect the papers. Get a copy of the FIR and keep every notice or document the police give you.
  2. Check the sections. The sections in the FIR decide whether the offence is bailable and which court will hear it.
  3. Speak to an advocate before you are arrested, if you can. Anticipatory bail is applied for before arrest, in the Sessions Court or High Court.
  4. Answer notices and attend when called. Keep copies of everything you sign or submit.

Since 1 July 2024, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) have replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act. Cases registered earlier may still refer to the old section numbers. What the new criminal laws change.

Cheque bounce cases: the time limits

  1. Present the cheque within its validity, which is three months from the date on it.
  2. The bank returns it unpaid and gives you a return memo.
  3. Send a legal notice within 30 days of receiving the memo, demanding payment.
  4. The drawer has 15 days from receiving the notice to pay.
  5. File the complaint within one month after those 15 days end, if payment is not made.

Under Sections 138 and 142 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. The cheque bounce process in Kadapa, step by step.

Can I get anticipatory bail in Kadapa?

If you fear arrest for a non-bailable offence, you can apply for anticipatory bail before the Sessions Court or the High Court under Section 482 of the BNSS (Section 438 of the old CrPC). Whether it is granted depends on the facts of the case. Bail and anticipatory bail in Kadapa.

Is a cheque bounce a criminal case?

Yes. Dishonour of a cheque for a debt is an offence under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, punishable with imprisonment of up to two years, a fine of up to twice the cheque amount, or both. Strict time limits apply to the notice and the complaint.

Do you handle NDPS cases?
